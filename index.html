Inside the book

The book

For the moment when “I’m fine” is no longer quite true.

Sit With It is a small, private place for the feelings that have not become sentences yet. It does not demand a morning routine, a breakthrough or a better version of you. It simply helps you notice what is here.

Some days that might mean three pages. Some days it might mean one honest word and closing the cover. Both count.

No streaks to maintain No right order No pressure to feel better

Open where you are

You do not need to read it from the beginning.

Choose the sentence that feels closest. The journal will meet you there.

01

I’m spiralling

When every thought brings three more and your body needs somewhere solid to return to.

02

I feel numb

When nothing seems to reach you and forcing a feeling would only push you further away.

03

Something’s moving

When you can feel a change before you can explain it, and need space to hear what it is asking.

04

I’m ready to let go

When holding on has become heavier than the thing itself, and release can happen a little at a time.

How to use it

One page. One breath. One honest thing.

There is no programme to complete. Just a simple way in when everything feels too loud—or too far away.

  1. 01Find your state

    Open to the section that sounds most like the truth today.

  2. 02Come back to the body

    Use the short grounding cue before asking yourself to explain anything.

  3. 03Answer what you can

    Write a page, a sentence or a single word. Stop when it feels enough.

This is a companion, not a cure.

It will not diagnose you, hurry you or replace professional support. It is simply somewhere to be honest with yourself between one moment and the next.

To give or keep

For the words you cannot quite find

A thoughtful thing to give when “let me know if you need anything” is not enough.

Give it to someone in a season of change, grief, uncertainty or quiet overwhelm. Or keep it close for yourself—the bedside-drawer version of a friend who does not need you to make sense before they sit down.

  • A warm, tactile paperback designed to be written in
  • Four emotional entry points rather than a rigid sequence
  • Grounding cues, original prompts and space for what comes
  • A dedication page for gifting it to somebody you love

A note from Rose

“I made the book I wanted when I could not think my way out of how I felt.”

I am very good at making sense of complicated things. But feelings are not operational problems, and sometimes trying harder only takes us further away from ourselves.

Sit With It began as a way to stay beside an experience without turning it into a task. I hope it feels less like being instructed and more like being accompanied.
